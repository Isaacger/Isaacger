## 1. Executive Summary

A phishing alert was generated by the Exchange security monitoring system under detection rule SOC282 – Deceptive Mail Detected (Event ID 257).

The alert involved an email with the subject **"Free Coffee Voucher"**, originating from `free@coffeeshooop.com` and addressed to `Felix@letsdefend.io`.

The message was allowed by the security control and contained a ZIP attachment. Initial analysis identified suspicious email content and a potentially deceptive sender domain. Further investigation is required to establish the attachment's behaviour and whether the recipient interacted with the message.

## 7. Analysis & Findings

Initial evidence indicates a suspicious email attempting to attract the recipient through a promotional coffee voucher offer.

The presence of a ZIP attachment increases the need for further inspection, particularly because the email was allowed by the security control.

### MITRE ATT&CK Mapping

| Technique | Description | Investigation relevance |
|---|---|---|
| T1566 | Phishing | Initial email-based delivery |
| T1566.002 | Spearphishing Link | Only applicable if a malicious link is confirmed |
| T1059 | Command and Scripting Interpreter | Requires evidence of suspicious command execution |
| T1204 | User Execution | Requires evidence of user interaction or execution |

**Additional relevant technique:** T1566.001 – Spearphishing Attachment may be more appropriate if the ZIP is confirmed as the malicious delivery mechanism.

### Current Findings

- Suspicious sender and promotional email content identified.
- ZIP attachment present.
- Email security action recorded as Allowed.
- No confirmed evidence of attachment execution.
- No confirmed evidence of credential theft or account compromise.

## 8. Verdict & Justification

**Provisional Verdict: Suspicious – Suspected Phishing**

The alert contains multiple suspicious indicators, including the deceptive promotional subject, ZIP attachment and allowed delivery action.

However, the available evidence is insufficient to establish whether the attachment is malicious or whether the recipient's system was compromised.

Final True Positive classification requires further validation of the attachment, email indicators and related activity.

## 9. Response & Containment

- [ ] Verify whether the email reached the recipient's mailbox.
- [ ] Search for other recipients and duplicate messages.
- [ ] Analyse the ZIP attachment in an isolated sandbox.
- [ ] Extract and validate malicious indicators.
- [ ] Quarantine or purge the email if confirmed malicious.
- [ ] Block confirmed malicious sender/domain/IP indicators.
- [ ] Check recipient interaction and endpoint telemetry.
- [ ] Reset credentials and revoke sessions if credential compromise is confirmed.
- [ ] Escalate to Tier 2 / Incident Response if malicious execution or compromise is identified.

## 10. Detection & Prevention Recommendations

- Improve detection of suspicious promotional emails and deceptive sender domains.
- Enable SPF, DKIM and DMARC enforcement where appropriate.
- Apply attachment inspection and sandboxing for ZIP archives.
- Review email gateway policies for suspicious compressed attachments.
- Monitor URL clicks and attachment execution.
- Correlate Exchange events with endpoint and identity telemetry.
- Strengthen user awareness of unexpected promotional offers and attachments.

## 11. Lessons Learned

- An email marked as Allowed should not automatically be considered safe.
- Email headers provide valuable information about sender authenticity and routing.
- ZIP attachments require additional inspection before determining their risk.
- MITRE ATT&CK mappings must reflect observed behaviour, not just alert metadata.
- Correlating email activity with endpoint telemetry is essential for identifying potential compromise.
- Evidence and timestamps must be validated before reaching a final verdict.
